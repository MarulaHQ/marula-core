# How to use the client portal

> **Audience:** both — the steps are yours as a client. Your service desk hands
> you the start (see their [portal setup page](admin-portal-setup.md)).

The **client portal** is where you open support requests, follow the work your
provider is delivering, read the bills they have issued, find the guides they
published for your team, and book an appointment. It is a separate site from
your provider's own workspace, with your company's records and nobody else's.

## Prerequisites

- A portal login created for you by your service desk. There is no public
  "sign up" button, on purpose. Your provider creates the account and sends you
  the link.
- Your service desk's portal address. If you forget your password, ask them to
  reset it or send a fresh sign-in link.
- Access to the records they have chosen to share. A tab with nothing in it
  usually means they have not published anything there yet, not that the portal
  is broken.

## 1. Sign in

Open the portal address your provider gave you. The sign-in page is titled
**"Marula — client portal sign-in"** and says **"Support access provided by
your service desk"** at the bottom. Enter your email address and password, or
use the magic sign-in link they sent.

Once signed in, the top bar carries the tabs, in the order your provider chose:

**Tickets · Projects · Invoices · Quotes · Schedule · Help · Documentation**

The account menu carries the portal's own light and dark theme. It changes this
portal only. It does not change your provider's workspace or your email client.

## 2. Open and follow a request

**Tickets** lists your open and past requests. Press the new-request button,
describe what is wrong and submit. The request becomes a ticket on your
provider's board immediately, and it keeps the same number when they reply.

Open a request to read the conversation and add to it. Every message posted to
the portal thread is visible to you and to the technician. Your provider's
internal notes are not. That is what lets them work in the open without
thinking out loud in front of you.

## 3. Follow a project

**Projects** answers a different question from Tickets: what is being
delivered, where it has got to, and the date that matters. Each row names the
project, its status and its planned completion. A row highlighted with
**Waiting for your answer** needs you before the work can move.

Open a project to see:

- **How it is going** — the shared steps, with dates where the plan has them
  and **Dates to be confirmed** where it does not.
- **Updates** — the latest update opens first, with earlier ones beneath it.
- **Work is paused** — if it is, the reason your project manager marked safe to
  show.
- **This project is just getting started** — the plan has not been shared yet.
  Nothing there is a promise until it is.

### When a change needs your answer

A change to the agreed scope appears in a highlighted **We need your answer**
block. It names the change, says what it does to the amount (**No change to the
amount** when it does not), and says whether it moves the completion date.

Press **I agree** when the change is right. The portal records your
acknowledgement and tells your project manager. The plan can then move. If the
change is not right, do not press it — reply to your project manager through
Tickets and they will revise it. The portal records agreement, not a
negotiation.

## 4. Read your invoices and quotes

**Invoices** lists every bill your provider has issued, with what has been paid
and any open balance. The figures come from their billing ledger, not from a
copy typed into the portal.

**Quotes** holds proposals sent to you for approval. Open one to read the line
items and the expiry date, then [approve or decline it](client-quote-approval.md).
A decline asks for a reason. That reason goes back to the person who sent it.

## 5. Find an answer without raising a ticket

The portal has two reading surfaces, and they are fed by two different things:

- **Help** — the articles your provider published for clients. Search by the
  word you would use on the phone ("VPN", "password", "printer"). A search with
  no results offers a contact link rather than an empty page.
- **Documentation** — the documents and guides your provider marked visible to
  your company, each with the date it was last reviewed.

If the answer is not there, raise a request. That is useful signal for your
provider: a question asked twice is a help article waiting to be written.

## 6. Book an appointment

**Schedule** shows the appointment slots your provider has opened for on-site
or remote visits. Pick a slot and confirm. The booking appears on their
dispatch board straight away, so you are not waiting for a person to copy it
across.

## What you learned

- Your provider creates the account, and the portal has no public sign-up.
- Tickets are requests and their conversation. Projects are delivery and the
  date that matters.
- A change order waits for **I agree** before the plan can move.
- Invoices and quotes come from your provider's ledger, and a decline carries
  your reason back.
- Help is published articles, and Documentation is the shared documents.
- Schedule books a real slot on your provider's board.
