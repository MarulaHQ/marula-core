# How to use the portal (client-side tasks)

> **Audience:** both — the steps are yours as a client. Your service
desk hands you the start (see their [portal setup page](admin-portal-setup.md)).

## Prerequisites

- A provisioned portal account (your service desk provisions it — there
  is no self-serve sign-up),
- The portal URL of your service desk's deployment, distinct from the
  staff login. Forgot the password? Ask your service desk — "Your service
  desk provisions accounts and can reset this password" is the portal's
  own instruction. Do not expect a self-serve reset.

The portal is its own small product: four sections in the nav
(**Requests**, **Invoices**, **Knowledge**, **Schedule**), separate
sign-in shell, and isolation from staff systems.

## 1. Sign in at the portal shell

Open the portal URL. The portal shell says "Meridian — client portal
sign-in" and carries the footer "Support access provided by your service
desk". You are in the right place. First arrival, no public content, started by your
service desk's provisioning.

## 2. Submit and follow requests

**Requests** is where you open and follow service requests. Each request
becomes a ticket on your service desk's side, attributed to the portal
channel. You see your requests — not anyone else's, not other clients'.
Status moves as the desk works it. You never see the desk's internal
notes, which are flagged internal and never reach the portal.

## 3. Watch your invoices

**Invoices** shows the invoices your service desk has issued to your org,
what has been paid, and what is outstanding. Statements and payment
records arrive from the desk. The portal does not invent financial
records. It reflects your service desk's billing ledger.

## 4. Self-serve from Knowledge

**Knowledge** holds articles your service desk has **published** and
flagged **client-facing**. Search resolves only through that published,
client-visible gate — your client-visible answer set is deliberately the
one your service desk curates, not the whole internal KB.

## 5. Book a time on Schedule

**Schedule** exposes your service desk's public scheduling links —
pick a slot, book, and get the confirmation. The
[dispatch side](staff-dispatch.md) of your service desk sees the same
booking materialize on its board, so your booking lands where a human,
not only a bot, will land help.

## What you learned

- Four nav surfaces carry the whole portal: Requests, Invoices,
  Knowledge, Schedule.
- Requests are real tickets behind the scenes. Invoices mirror your
  desk's ledger. Knowledge shows only your client-facing subset, and
  Schedule writes bookings back into the desk's calendar.
- A model context on isolation (why "yours and only yours" is guaranteed
  rather than promised) is
  [portal isolation](../explanation/portal-isolation.md). Your service
  desk's own setup story (the MSP side) is in the
  [portal onboarding tutorial](../tutorials/portal-onboarding.md).
