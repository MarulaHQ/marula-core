# Why we're building Marula

I nearly got fired over time entry.

That's probably the honest place to start. I was doing the work, closing out tickets and putting out fires as any good tech does. One of our clients even named me in the most glowing Google review. Beneath all of that though, I was fighting for my life. 

Working a ticket took four separate screens, hitting the save button 5 or 6 times and having to wait with every response slowed down every process. Sometimes the time entry took longer than the actual fix, and the hours kept slipping until somebody noticed. Dispatchers, managers and approvers all had similar problems, and every team in the company had an expensive third party tool bolted on to try and cope with the inadequacy of the PSA we were using. 

I know you know what I'm talking about, because this was one of the most widely used PSAs in the world.

I knew there had to be a better way. And on the brink of losing my job and finding myself with a bout of hyperfocus, I decided to actually build it.

Walk with me and picture this:

A ticket takes one screen to work, not three. Notes, timer, conversation and docs live in the same view, because they are the same job. The timer follows you across tickets, and rough notes become a proper time entry with but a click. 

Mid-ticket and you need something to know something? You don't have to go search somewhere else, because the agent has already surfaced the relevant docs. And if you need even more context, you just ask. The agent reads from your knowledge base and the client's own documentation. It knows who the ticket is for, what is actually in that client's environment, and how your company handles this kind of problem. 

The agent isn't a chatbot bolted to the side either - it's that old L3 who's been here since the company was a three-man shop. Dispatch gets the same boost, with a fast and intelligent system-one triage system that makes suggestions based on what's actually happening with a ticket. Humans can make the human decisions, but AI makes the mechanical things go faster.

You can bring your own keys, select what model to use for every surface at all times, or if you prefer your existing agent just hook up the MCP server and you're off to the races. You can choose just how much AI you want (including none at all!) and how much you want to spend on it. You can have sovereignty and certainty about where your data is processed. And when something new and shiny comes out, you don't have to wait for anyone else to do something so that you can adopt it. 

Quoting, invoicing, time approvals, documentation, search, projects, workflows - every single surface should be fast, secure, use AI when it's helpful and just get out of the way when it's not. 

And, possibly most important of all, the core is open source. Contribute, host it yourself, fork it, break it, rebuild it. No lock-in, multi-year contracts, sh*tty business practices or private-equity pricing spirals. You can leave with your whole system on any given day and you can extend it any which way and make it entirely your own. I know the Rewst guy in your office is salivating thinking about the possibilities.

Speaking of the Rewst guy, your PSA is extensible whilst remaining secure. You can build a plugin for whatever you need - RMMs, billing, accounting, automation systems - anything that enhances your workflow by connecting deeply into your PSA and share it with the community.

With this project, this is no longer a pipe-dream. This letter is a long-winded way of saying that we don't want to see another tech suffer like we have. If no MSP ever has to see *that* PSA ever again, we'll have succeeded.

I hope you'll join us in making the IT world just that little bit better for the people that work in it.

With warm regards, and a not-so-fond wave to the PSAs of old
An aggrieved sysadmin.
