At a glance

Marula is a complete PSA and agentic operating system for managed service providers. The core is open source and free forever; the business sells hosting, managed operations, AI compute and painless migration on top of it.

What

Tickets, time, dispatch, projects, billing, knowledge and an assistant that knows each client.
Who for
MSPs of 5 to 100+ seats
New shops with no incumbent, and ConnectWise, Syncro, Autotask, Halo and Atera users ready to leave.
Business model
Per-seat subscription plus AI credits
Bring your own AI key and pay nothing for AI. Credit margin target of 60% or more.
Moat
Trust, migration and velocity
Readable, self-hostable code; a migration engine that sells confidence; a thin closed layer that moves fast.

In a private repo still called Project Meridian. First public commit to a Marula org is next.

Solo founder with AI agents, about two hires to first revenue. Raise only if a trigger fires.

02

Why we're building it

“I nearly got fired over time entry. The work was getting done. What I couldn't do was fight the software and win.”

Tumi Lengoasa, founder, from the Marula manifesto

Marula starts from a technician's frustration, not a market map. In the incumbent PSAs, adding a note to a ticket takes four screens, time entry is slower than the fixes it describes, and dispatch hides what a scheduler needs behind tabs nobody opens. Good techs end up looking like the problem.

The market problem

MSPs are emotionally ready to leave their incumbents. Two fears keep them where they are: migration fear (losing history, breaking billing, disrupting clients) and billing risk (a bad month-end during a switch). Meanwhile incumbents are bolting on AI features priced as upsells, on closed platforms nobody can inspect.

Our answer

A ticket takes one screen

Notes, timer, conversation and calendar live in the same view, because they're the same job. Rough notes become a proper time entry.

The assistant is the old L3

It reads your knowledge base and the client's own documentation, knows the client's environment, and does the mechanical work. People make the decisions.

You can always leave

The core is open source. Host it yourself, fork it, extend it, and export your whole system on any given day.

The positioning line, kept from the original spec: the PSA that MSP technicians would build for themselves.

03

What we're building

Not "a PSA with AI features". A complete PSA that is also a harness safe enough to run agents against real MSP operations.

A complete PSA, with no required subscriptions

A full MSP can run on Marula Core alone. That completeness is deliberate: it lets new MSPs adopt us without an incumbent to migrate from, and it kills the "just a ConnectWise skin" objection.

The PSA as an agent harness

Every agent needs five things. Marula provides each of them natively, which is what makes the assistant trustworthy rather than a chatbot bolted to the side.

Tools

The domain API itself, exposed internally and as an MCP server.

Skills and rules

Versioned playbooks plus layered guardrails: platform, organisation, role, session.

Memory

The event bus and a retrieval corpus of knowledge, tickets and client context.

Accountability

An append-only audit of every action, human or agent, with full provenance.

Where people use it

A web app built on Next.js, a desktop app (Tauri) with a tray timer and multi-window dispatch, and a mobile field-tech experience that starts as a PWA and moves to React Native when field usage justifies it. The designs for the web workbench and the mobile app live on the Marula identity canvas.

Editions and pricing

One product, one name, editions that differ by who runs it and how much managed help you get. Prices are drafts to validate with closed-beta MSPs.

marulaCore
Free, forever (AGPL)

The full open core, self-hosted, bring your own AI key, community support. For DIY MSPs, tinkerers and contributors.

marulaSelf-Hosted
Same per-seat price as Cloud

The paid capabilities on your own infrastructure: signed offline licence, marketplace packs, consented remote support. No hosting premium.

marulaCloud
Pro ~$79–99 · Business ~$129–159 per seat/mo

Hosted and managed. Pro for 5–50 seats; Business adds the migration engine, higher credit allowances, priority support and sandboxes.

marulaEnterprise
Custom, annual

50+ seats and regulated MSPs: SAML and SCIM, residency, dedicated database or region, compliance artefacts, full white-label.

AI credits

Credits are metered by task class. Local transcription is free, simple tasks cost next to nothing, and frontier-model work is billed per credit. Customers who bring their own key pay nothing for AI. That is a deliberate wedge against incumbents' AI pricing and a trust signal.

Licensing is a product value, not fine print

  • Licences are signed and verifiable offline.
  • A lapse degrades paid capability after a grace window and never touches data.
  • The open core never enforces commercial licensing.
  • Remote support is consented, logged and revocable.
  • Customers can move from Cloud to Self-Hosted without losing anything, which is what makes the hosted business trustworthy.

Other revenue lines

Managed migration projects at Enterprise; premium marketplace connectors included with paid editions; and later, a revenue share on payments MSPs collect from their own clients through native invoicing. The marketplace takes no cut of community extensions at first: ecosystem growth matters more than 30% of nothing.

The gatekeeping rule

A feature belongs in the open core if removing it would break an MSP's daily operation. It becomes paid only if it rides hosting, managed operation or third-party AI compute. You pay for operations, compute and transition, never for an operating feature the free product already runs.

The biggest licensing risk is internal, not external: scope creep of the open core into paid territory. Every capability added to the core is one any competitor inherits for free, so this rule gets enforced ruthlessly.

Why AGPL holds up

For a vertical application, "someone will host our code and undercut us" collapses into ordinary competition: to win a hosted MSP, a clone would also need migrations, working OAuth apps, support and compliance answers, which are exactly our closed surfaces. AGPL adds a protection restrictive licences don't: no incumbent can ever absorb the core and quietly sunset it. The written legal opinion on the AGPL and plugin boundary is a blocker before public launch.

Community

The repo goes public during the public-beta phase, with an RFC process, a good-first-issue pipeline, community calls and roadmap voting. The plugin and connector SDK is the on-ramp for contributors. The core must succeed as a product even with zero contributors, and it's designed to.

Tenancy

Marula Core supports multi-tenancy at the data layer; the paid Cloud edition is where multi-tenancy becomes a product. In Cloud, each tenant is a whole Marula deployment with its own database, which makes isolation structural rather than a shared row-space.

Unit economics

The biggest lever is architectural: in Cloud, only the 4–5 application images run per tenant, while Postgres, cache, storage, mail, observability and inference become shared regional services. That shrinks a tenant from about 16 images and 9 GB to about 5 images and 2 GB.

Prices are September 2026 list prices and get re-checked before each decision. Per-tenant platforms like Fly or Render would push hosting to 15–40% of revenue: fine at 3 tenants, ruinous at 300.

Durability promises

  • Nightly base backups plus point-in-time recovery, with regular restore drills.
  • Suspension pauses capability but never locks data; export always works.
  • Upgrades go out in canary rings scored by risk, respecting each MSP's month-end.
  • Self-hosters get the same backup tooling (WAL-G and restic) and a rehearsed restore script.
08

AI services

We are not a markup on a gateway. The business is reselling inference and operating it well.

LiteLLM is open-source software we host ourselves, so there's no licence to mark up. Customers pay us for credits; we pay model providers or run our own GPUs; we keep the spread. A thin processing margin would be a commodity with no moat and would go negative the first time an expensive fallback model fired. So the margin is earned:

Routing intelligence

Each task goes to the cheapest model that clears the quality bar for its class.

Caching and context discipline

Per-tenant caching and compression cut tokens without cutting quality.

Local inference

Bundled Whisper transcription and embeddings at compute-only cost.

Governance you can't buy

No-training enforcement, residency, audit and a policy receipt for every request.

Retrieval operations

Managed retrieval pipelines over each MSP's knowledge, a deliberate core non-goal.

Billing convenience

One invoice, credits, and no provider accounts to manage.

Launch blocker. Reselling inference needs partner or reseller terms with each provider, not just an API key, plus data processing agreements. No managed-credit product goes live until those are signed.
09

The Transition Engine

Migration is the wedge. We sell confidence, not features.

The goal: a new customer runs their full operation in Marula within weeks of signing up, with zero client impact and zero billing disruption. Clean ConnectWise or Syncro sources can land in about two weeks; complex 15-year histories realistically take two to six, mostly elapsed ingestion and parallel running rather than labour. We publish honest ranges per source, because over-promising destroys the confidence we sell.

Extract

API extraction with checkpointed, resumable cursors that respect incumbent rate limits.

Map

AI-assisted mapping packs; the admin makes the decisions.

Verify and run in parallel

A confidence dashboard and a parallel run against live feeds before anyone commits.

Cut over

Cutover with rollback and a post-migration audit. Nothing is ever deleted or overwritten.

What's open and what's paid

The open interchange format, generic importer and exporter, and provenance stamping on every migrated record are all in the open core: arriving should be as trustworthy as leaving. The vendor-specific connectors and mapping packs for ConnectWise and Syncro first, then Autotask, HaloPSA and Atera, are the paid wedge. Guided self-serve migration is included in Cloud Business; managed and white-glove migrations are priced per project at Enterprise.

10

Go-to-market

Motion 1 · primary

Complete product plus migration wedge

New and small MSPs adopt a complete PSA at a fair price, and they're the volume engine. Frustrated incumbent users come through the migration calculator, parallel-run content and the confidence dashboard demo. Founder-led sales until about 50 customers.

Motion 2 · compounding

Open-source community

Public repo, RFCs, community calls and the SDK as on-ramp. The core is free forever; convenience is paid. The GitLab and Sentry playbook, not open core in name only.

Motion 3 · later

Marketplace flywheel

Every connector a community developer builds makes Marula stickier without founder time. Verified and paid listings come later.

Early customers

Alpha MSPs have a 90-day success definition: all new tickets flowing through Marula in a parallel run, at least 80% of billable time logged in-product, a written list of their top ten blocking gaps, and one champion willing to be a public reference. Closed-beta MSPs get lifetime-locked pricing in exchange for the usage data that sets tier boundaries.

Web presence

Waitlist first, live marketing site second, with the domain and handle shortlist on the Web presence page of the identity canvas. The brand voice guidelines (warm, plain, a little wry, has your back) govern every surface.

Roadmap and gates

Phases are gates, not dates. A phase advances when its exit criteria are met, and months count from the first public commit (M0).

We're ahead of the plan. The month windows below come from the planning pack, which assumed building from scratch. The core is already about 90% built privately, so the build-heavy early phases should compress. The gates stay exactly as written; the dates get re-baselined at M0.

Phase 0 · Conceptualisation

Complete

Planning documents, the open and closed boundary, the stack and the commercial hypothesis. Plus a private core build well beyond the plan.

Phase 1 · Private alpha

M1–M5 · v0.1–v0.4

Move the codebase into a Marula GitHub org, rename it, and run real work with two affiliated MSPs.

Gate: core loop live with no data loss; two MSPs doing real work.

Phase 2 · Closed beta

M5–M10 · v0.5–v0.7

The daily driver: email-to-ticket, SLAs, dispatch, portal, plugins. Managed AI router and the first migration connectors start. Waitlists go live.

Gate: email-to-ticket clean for two weeks, SLAs proven, 5–10 MSPs active.

Phase 3 · Public beta

M10–M16 · v0.8–v0.9

The commercial engine, QuickBooks and Xero sync, the first hosted cell and control plane. The repo goes public.

Gate: accounting sync verified, one live ConnectWise migration, external contributors.

Phase 4 · First revenue

M14–M20

Metering, then Paddle monetisation, self-serve checkout and first paying-customer SLAs.

Gate: paying customers on record, accurate margin meter.

Phase 5 · General availability

M16–M22 · v1.0

Performance, external security audit, marketplace v1, EU cell and cross-region recovery.

Gate: clean audit, 3+ live migrations, 30 days with no critical data bugs.

Phase 6 · Ecosystem and enterprise

M22–M30 · v1.x

SSO and SCIM, sandboxes, SOC 2 Type I (around M24), marketplace v2, Rewst bridge and Slack and Teams apps.

Gate: enterprise pipeline live; SOC 2 Type II underway.

Phase 7 · v2.0 horizon

M30+

Each item waits for its trigger: native mobile apps when field-tech usage passes 30%, procurement, multi-organisation federation, BI and a workflow builder.

The critical path runs through the core phases in order, with the control plane and retrieval work in parallel during public beta. First revenue waits on the chain of control plane, metering and monetisation.

Agents compress implementation. They don't compress design review, operations, sales or trust-building, which is why the hiring triggers sit where they do.

Funding

Bootstrap first. Revenue from around M16–M20 funds hires. An external raise happens only if the enterprise motion pulls forward and needs compliance spend, or a competitor's raise changes the clock. Open-source traction is the raise story if we need one.

Legal structure

The founder is a South African tax resident building a USD-revenue business, so SARS income tax, SARB exchange control and the controlled-foreign-company rules shape everything. The recommended ladder:

Now

A single SA Pty Ltd

Founder-owned, developing and taking early revenue through Paddle. Register trademarks in SA and the US, then the EU. DCO, not CLA. Apply for Section 11D R&D pre-approval.

Decide before commercial IP

The offshore IP question

Moving IP offshore later is the most expensive step in the plan. If an offshore route is ever wanted, the commercial IP should be created offshore from the start.

At first raise

The raise picks the topco

US investors point to a Delaware topco, which also clears the CFC test outright. Bootstrapping to scale points to a Mauritius holdco with the SA company as the development engine.

Time-sensitive. Because the core is already largely built, whether the paid-edition code is created in SA or offshore needs deciding before significant commercial IP is written. This is a question for a tax adviser, not a default.
